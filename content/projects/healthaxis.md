## Overview

HealthAxis was a self-initiated concept project exploring how product design can meaningfully improve the delivery of patient care in environments where hospital management is still largely manual, siloed, and paper-dependent.

As Lead Designer I owned the research process, information architecture, UI design, and usability testing, collaborating with Emmanuela Chidiuto Kelechi to define and execute the full product concept. The brief we set for ourselves was to design a platform that genuinely serves three very different user types — patients, doctors, and administrators — without compromising the needs of any one group in favour of another.

Collaborated with medical professionals to design a clinical-grade interface that reduces cognitive load for busy healthcare workers.

Unified patient and hospital management system reducing healthcare fragmentation and improving patient care.

Built on deep user research and validated through Maze testing, it replaces fragmented hospital workflows with a single, accessible, and intuitive platform.

## Problem

Hospital management in many markets, particularly across Africa, operates with significant structural gaps that directly affect the quality of care patients receive:

- **Fragmented patient records** — medical histories, prescriptions, test results, and appointment records are stored across disconnected systems or physical files, making it difficult for doctors to make well-informed decisions quickly
- **Communication breakdown** — patients have little to no visibility into their care status, appointment changes, or test results, creating anxiety and eroding trust in the healthcare system
- **Administrative overload** — hospital administrators manage scheduling, billing, staff coordination, and compliance manually, leading to errors, delays, and burnout
- **No unified coordination layer** — doctors, nurses, and administrative staff operate from separate tools with no shared view of patient status, creating dangerous information gaps at critical moments
- **Accessibility barriers** — existing hospital management systems are often designed for IT-literate users, leaving clinical staff who aren't technology-native underserved and resistant to adoption

Fragmented hospital systems led to data silos, duplicated medical tests, and slow patient turnaround times.

## Solution

The design process was grounded in user research from the outset, with every major decision validated against real user feedback before moving to high-fidelity execution.

**Key design decisions:**

- **Research-led foundation** — the project began with in-depth user research to identify pain points across all three user groups, ensuring the information architecture reflected how patients, doctors, and administrators actually think and work, not how a developer assumed they would
- **Role-based dashboard architecture** — designed distinct dashboard experiences for patients, doctors, and administrators within a unified system, giving each user type a purpose-built environment without building three separate products
- **Unified patient records** — the core design challenge was making a patient's complete medical history, appointments, prescriptions, and test results instantly accessible to any authorised clinician, reducing time-to-decision in clinical settings
- **Transparent patient communication** — designed a patient-facing portal with real-time appointment status, test result notifications, and care updates, directly addressing the trust and visibility gap that erodes patient confidence
- **Streamlined administrative workflows** — scheduling, billing, and staff coordination were designed as automated, template-driven flows rather than manual processes, reducing administrative error and cognitive load
- **Accessibility-first UI** — clean, high-contrast interface with clear typographic hierarchy and minimal interaction complexity, specifically designed for clinical staff who need to act quickly under pressure
- **Maze usability testing** — design decisions were validated through structured usability testing on Maze, with iteration cycles improving task completion rates and reducing navigation friction before final delivery

Developed a unified SaaS platform where patient records, billing, and pharmacy management are synchronized in one view.

## Impact

Designed to handle 10,000+ concurrent users with sub-second data retrieval times.

- **Maze usability testing completed** with real user feedback driving measurable improvements to navigation and task flow efficiency
- Successfully demonstrated a unified design pattern for multi-stakeholder healthcare platforms that balances clinical precision with accessible, non-intimidating UI
- Validated the information architecture across three distinct user mental models — patients, doctors, and administrators — within a single coherent system
