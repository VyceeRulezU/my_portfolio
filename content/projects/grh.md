## Overview

Designed the complete UI/UX from public-facing marketing pages to full student and admin dashboards. Delivered a system spanning course discovery, AI content search, and a resource library.

PERL (Policy and Legal Advocacy Centre), a subsidiary of DAI and UKAID-sponsored programme, needed a centralised digital platform to make governance education accessible to citizens, civil society organisations, and policy advocates across Nigeria. The existing landscape was fragmented — resources were scattered, inaccessible to non-specialists, and offered no structured learning pathway for users engaging with governance topics for the first time.

I was brought on as the end-to-end Lead Designer and UX Engineer, owning the project from initial discovery through to live deployment.

## Problem

Governance education was fragmented and inaccessible to the general public, leading to low civic engagement and awareness.

Governance education in Nigeria suffered from a visibility and accessibility gap. Key challenges identified during the discovery phase:

- Fragmentation: Resources existed across dozens of disconnected sources with no single authoritative hub
- Low civic engagement: Citizens had no structured entry point into governance content, leading to low awareness and participation
- No learning infrastructure: There was no platform that combined structured courses, resource libraries, and real-time policy insights in one place
- Accessibility barriers: Existing platforms were desktop-heavy, jargon-dense, and not designed for Nigeria's diverse digital literacy levels

## Solution

We built a centralized, AI-enhanced platform that provides structured courses, real-time resources, and interactive assessments for citizens and policymakers.

The solution was a unified governance education platform built to serve three distinct user groups — learners, researchers, and policy advocates — without overwhelming any of them.

Key design decisions:

1. **Information architecture first**: Conducted a full content audit and sitemap before any visual design, ensuring the platform's structure reflected how users actually seek governance information.
1. **Progressive disclosure**: Designed learning pathways that surface complexity gradually, reducing cognitive load for first-time users
1. **Component-based design system**: Built a scalable Figma design system that mapped 1:1 to the React component library, eliminating handoff friction.
1. **Mobile-first approach**: Prioritized responsive layouts given Nigeria's predominantly mobile internet usage patterns.
1. **Iterative usability testing**: Ran multiple rounds of internal and external review cycles over the 4–6 month engagement, refining navigation, course discovery, and resource filtering based on direct stakeholder feedback.

The platform was built on React 18, Vite, and Supabase — chosen for performance, scalability, and real-time data capabilities.

## Impact

The Governance Resource Hub launched as a fully functional platform delivering measurable results:

- 1,600+ governance resources catalogued and accessible in a structured, searchable library
- Live video course streaming — class sessions functional and stable at launch with no performance degradation
- Near-zero bounce rate degradation post-launch, indicating strong content-to-user fit and effective information architecture
- 38% increase in user engagement following launch
- 4–6 month delivery — from discovery through external review, stakeholder sign-off, and live deployment
- Platform serves citizens, CSOs, and policy advocates across Nigeria under a UKAID-funded mandate
- The live platform saw a 98% increase in visits post-launch with near-zero bounce rate degradation.
